# Parameter estimation of a whole-brain model with a hybrid physics-informed neural network

## 1. Objective and scope

We show that the regional excitabilities $\eta_i$ and the global coupling $G$ of a Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin (MPR) whole-brain model, estimated with a hybrid physics-informed neural network (PINN), reproduce the resting-state BOLD of a real subject and can be interpreted as properties of that brain. The claim is one of inference, which is stronger than a good fit: a parameter set that reproduces the data is not enough if the data do not determine it.

Scope of version 1: one subject of 1000BRAINS v1.1, Desikan–Killiany atlas with $N=70$ regions, $T=300$ volumes at TR $=2.2$ s (Section 3.1). Only $\eta_i$ and $G$ are estimated; every other parameter is fixed and declared. Using the estimated parameters to explore perturbations that could inform the treatment of disorders is future work and motivation, not a result of this proposal.

Decisions that depend on forward simulations (the value of $G_0$ and the useful range of $G$, the check of the noise level, the scale $\chi$ of the Balloon input, heterogeneous $\eta_i$ in the generator) are not fixed here. They belong to the simulation stages of the project and are listed in Section 7.

## 2. Hypotheses

These hypotheses supersede the numbering of revision r01.

- **H1 (existence).** There exist $\eta_i$ and $G$, obtained with the PINN, such that integrating the MPR model forward with them and passing the result through the Balloon–Windkessel model and the BOLD equation yields a functional connectivity (FC) that matches the real FC. Success is measured by $\rho_{\mathrm{FC}}$ against the ceilings of Section 6, not against the structural reference $\rho(\mathrm{SC},\mathrm{FC})=0.28$, which is only a lower reference.
- **H2 (identifiability on real data).** The data determine the parameters: repeated training with different seeds and different initial values of $\eta_i$ and $G$ converges to the same values. If runs reach different values with the same FC, the parameters are not identifiable and they are not interpreted.
- **H3 (optional, recovery on synthetic data).** With synthetic data generated from known $\eta_i$ and $G$ chosen away from the centre of the prior, the method recovers them. This needs a forward simulation used only as a data generator.

## 3. Model

Each region $i$ carries one MPR neural mass, coupled through the structural connectome $A$ (fibre counts without diagonal, normalised to a maximum of 1), without delays in version 1:

$$
\tau_m \dot r_i = \frac{\Delta}{\pi \tau_m} + 2 r_i V_i \tag{1}
$$

$$
\tau_m \dot V_i = V_i^2 + \eta_i - (\pi \tau_m r_i)^2 + \tau_m J r_i + \tau_m G \sum_j A_{ij} r_j \tag{2}
$$

with fixed $\Delta=0.7$ and $J=14.5$, taken from Rabuffo et al. (2021), who fit them so that an isolated region is bistable. The time constant is $\tau_m=1$ ms. Rabuffo et al. use a dimensionless characteristic time $\tau_c=1$ and an integration step of 1 ms ("2000 steps are 2 s"), so one unit of model time corresponds to 1 ms, and Hashemi et al. (2024) work with the same model and the same convention of regions switching between states. The value $\tau_m=1$ ms is therefore a declared assumption supported by that coherence, not a published measurement. A value of 10 ms is used as a sensitivity test. The initial value of $\eta_i$ is $-4.6$, the homogeneous value of Rabuffo et al., inside the bistable range $(-5.35,-2.45)$ that was computed here for $\Delta=0.7$ and $J=14.5$ and is not reported in that paper. $G_0$ is the inverse of the mean row sum of $A$, a unit of scale and not a value taken from the literature.

**Noise.** The PINN has no explicit noise term: the residual of Eq. (2) absorbs it. Forward simulations used as data generators and for the checks of Section 6 add a Gaussian noise $\xi_i$ of zero mean to Eq. (2), in the same position as Hashemi et al. (2024, Section 2.2, Eq. 1 and Figure S1), who state $\xi(t)\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ with variance $\sigma^2=0.03$ and report that, with this noise, the regions are in the bistable regime around $G=0.5$. This value is inherited, fixed and declared; it is not estimated, and the same value is used wherever the noise level enters, so the simulated and trained quantities are comparable. Three things are verified and three are not. Verified: the time step of the source model is 1 ms with $\tau_c=1$ (Rabuffo et al. 2021), the convention adopted here for $\tau_m$; the documentation of The Virtual Brain, the software of both papers, defines its additive noise parameter `nsig` as a standard deviation, not a variance; and its stochastic examples for this model use a stochastic Heun integrator. Not verified: whether Hashemi et al. passed $0.03$ to the software as a variance or as `nsig`, which differ by a factor of about 5.8 in amplitude ($\sqrt{0.03}\approx0.17$ against $0.03$ read as a standard deviation); the integrator and step they used; and how the integrator scales the noise with the step. Hashemi et al. also couple the regions diffusively, $G\sum_j SC_{ij}(r_j-r_i)$, whereas Eq. (2) couples through the additive current $G\sum_j A_{ij} r_j$; this difference is declared and not resolved. The sensitivity of the results to the noise is reported over a grid that covers both readings of $0.03$.

The firing rate drives a Balloon–Windkessel model (Friston et al. 2000) through $z_i=\chi(r_i-\bar r_i)$. Its parameters are those of the reference implementation of dynamic causal modelling, SPM12 (`spm_fx_fmri.m`), which coincide with Zeidman et al. (2019, Table 3) where that table lists them: $\kappa=0.64$ s$^{-1}$, $\gamma=0.32$ s$^{-1}$, $\tau_h=2.00$ s, $\alpha=0.32$ and $E_0=0.4$. The value of $\gamma$ is taken from the code, because the table of Zeidman et al. does not list it. These are the defaults of an estimation tool, not measurements. The values of Friston et al. (2003, Table 1), $\kappa=0.65$ s$^{-1}$, $\gamma=0.41$ s$^{-1}$, $\tau_h=0.98$ s, $\alpha=0.32$ and $E_0=0.34$, which Heinzle et al. (2016) also use, are the sensitivity case. None of the sources read here gives the reason for the change from the second set to the first, so it is reported as undocumented. The effect of $\kappa$, $\gamma$, $\tau_h$ and $E_0$ is covered by a sensitivity analysis. The scale $\chi$ depends on the scale of $r$, which changes with $\tau_m$, and is set in the simulation stage.

The BOLD signal is

$$
y_i = V_0\left[k_1(1-q_i) + k_2\left(1-\frac{q_i}{\nu_i}\right) + k_3(1-\nu_i)\right] \tag{3}
$$

with $k_1 = 4.3\,\vartheta_0 E_0\,\mathrm{TE}$, $k_2 = \varepsilon\,\varrho_0 E_0\,\mathrm{TE}$ and $k_3 = 1-\varepsilon$ (Stephan et al. 2007, Eq. B9; Heinzle et al. 2016, Eq. 7). The scanner of 1000BRAINS is a 3 T Siemens Tim-TRIO with TE $=30$ ms (Caspers et al. 2014). For 3 T and that TE, Heinzle et al. (Appendix A) give $\vartheta_0\approx 28.265\,B_0\approx 84.8$ s$^{-1}$, $\varrho_0\approx 110$ s$^{-1}$ and $\varepsilon\approx 0.47$, none of which depends on $E_0$. With $E_0=0.4$ they yield $k_1\approx 4.38$, $k_2\approx 0.62$ and $k_3=0.53$; with $E_0=0.34$ the same inputs would give $3.72$, $0.53$ and $0.53$. These three numbers are computed here from published values; they are not published. Heinzle et al. fix $E_0=0.34$ themselves, so the pairing of $E_0$ from SPM12 with their field coefficients is a combination made in this work. The ratio $\varepsilon$ is the least certain input: Obata et al. fixed 1.43 at 1.5 T, Buxton et al. assumed 0.4, and Stephan et al. (2007) propose to treat it as a free parameter with prior mean 1. Here the 3 T value of Heinzle et al. is fixed. The resting blood volume fraction is $V_0=0.02$ (Friston et al. 2003); it only scales the signal and does not affect a correlation. The sensitivity of the FC to the $k_i$ is tested.

### 3.1 Data

One subject of 1000BRAINS v1.1 (Jung et al. 2023): Desikan–Killiany parcellation with 70 regions, an EPI acquisition of 11 minutes with TR $=2.2$ s, hence $T=300$ volumes. The dataset FC is the Pearson correlation between the regional BOLD series after linear detrending and a nuisance regression that includes the global signal, so global signal regression is already in the data and is applied to the simulated BOLD as well. 88 of the 261 subjects of the dataset have a second session; whether this subject is one of them has not been confirmed.

The simulated and measured FC are compared directly, as Pearson correlations, over the 2415 pairs of the upper triangle. The Fisher $z$ transformation is not applied; it is tested as a sensitivity analysis.

## 4. Method

A single fully connected network with three hidden layers of 100 tanh units, Glorot initialisation, and time $t$ scaled to $[-1,1]$ as input maps time to $(\hat r_1,\dots,\hat r_N,\hat V_1,\dots,\hat V_N)$, with a softplus on $\hat r$ so that the rate stays positive. The depth and width follow the PINNs of Raissi et al. (2019, 4–5 layers of 50–200 units) and the PINN of resting-state dynamics of 2024 (three layers of 100 tanh units, Glorot); the output, with 140 variables, is the only difference. The last hidden layer has 100 units and the output 140 variables, so the trajectories are linear combinations of 100 functions, which limits the patterns the network can represent. The effect of a wider layer is tested.

The derivatives come from automatic differentiation. The network weights $\varphi$, $\eta$ and $G$ are trained jointly. The network output feeds a differentiable RK4 integration of the Balloon–Windkessel model, so that the BOLD and its FC can be compared with the data.

**Parametrisation.** $\eta_i$ and $G$ are parametrised as dimensionless deviations from the scales already used by the prior, $\eta_i=-4.6+s_\eta\,\tilde\eta_i$ and $G=G_0+s_G\,\tilde G$, so that a single learning rate means a comparable relative step for both.

**Optimisation.** Adam with initial learning rate $10^{-3}$ and a stepwise decay, as in the PINN of Pinning the Balloon (2026; $10^{-3}$, decay factor 0.15 every 1000 iterations) and the PINN of resting-state dynamics (2024; $10^{-3}$, 40 000 iterations). A final polish with L-BFGS in full batch is optional: Raissi et al. use it when the training set has hundreds to thousands of points, as here ($K=1000$), and Rathore et al. (2024) report that Adam followed by L-BFGS outperforms either alone.

**Stopping.** The number of iterations is fixed by a pilot run and declared; it is not chosen a priori. Training stops early when both the loss and the parameters $\eta_i$ and $G$ have stabilised, and the full trajectories of $\eta_i$, $G$ and the loss are recorded. A run that reaches the iteration cap, or whose loss plateaus while the parameters are still moving, is marked as not converged and is not interpreted. The thresholds of "stable" are set with the pilot run. Stopping on the loss alone would have accepted the first run (Section 7), where the loss stabilised while the parameters had not moved.

Two-phase schedules and different learning rates for $\eta$ and $G$ are not adopted: they have no support in the references of this work, and are evaluated only if the pilot run shows that $\eta$ and $G$ do not move.

## 5. Loss function

The loss combines three terms:

$$
\mathcal L = \lambda_{\mathrm{phys}} \tilde{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{phys}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{data}} \tilde{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{data}} + \lambda_\theta \mathcal L_\theta \tag{4}
$$

Each term is divided by a fixed scale with a meaning defined by the problem, never by a value taken from the current state of training. Dividing by the running value of another loss makes the weight collapse as the network improves, which is what happened in the first run.

- **Physics.** The equations are non-dimensionalised before the loss is built, which is the first step of the training pipeline of "An Expert's Guide to Training Physics-informed Neural Networks" (arXiv 2308.08468) and the scaling of Theodosiou and Rekatsinas (2025, Section 2.2): Eqs. (1) and (2) are written in dimensionless form with $\tau_m$ as the only time scale. The residual of each variable is then divided by a fixed constant $s_j$ computed before training, as in the physics term of PINODE-SR (2025), where $s_j$ is the empirical standard deviation of the derivative of variable $j$ in the training window, and as in Pinning the Balloon (2026), where a scalar weight levels the residuals to the magnitude of the data term. Here only the BOLD is observed, so $r$ and $V$ have no data to compute $s_j$ from: it is derived from the characteristic magnitude of the terms of the equations, or from a forward simulation in the simulation stage. Which of the two, and the numerical value, are not yet fixed. SEE-PINN is demonstrated only on one-dimensional problems of mechanics, and the Expert's Guide treats forward problems of partial differential equations; applying either to this coupled system of 140 variables with parameters to estimate is an extension made in this work.
- **Data.** The squared error between simulated and real FC over the 2415 upper-triangle pairs, divided by the variance of the real FC entries, which is the error of the constant predictor equal to the mean. It reads as the fraction of the FC variance left unexplained. The term $1-\rho_{\mathrm{FC}}$ is already dimensionless.
- **Prior.** Gaussian, $\sum_i (\eta_i+4.6)^2/(2 s_\eta^2) + (G-G_0)^2/(2 s_G^2)$ with $s_\eta=1$ and $s_G=0.5\,G_0$, with $\lambda_\theta=1$.

With the terms normalised, each $\lambda$ is chosen in $[0,1]$, and the proposal reports a sensitivity analysis over a grid of values. Deriving the weights from noise levels, as in Bayesian PINNs (Yang et al. 2021) and in uncertainty-based loss weighting (Kendall et al. 2018), is a later refinement: the physics weight would follow from the intensity $\sigma$ of the noise that the residual of Eq. (2) is read to represent, and the data weight from the natural variability of the FC. That choice is a design decision of this work, not an established practice for FC.

**Alternative.** Adaptive weights, which change during training (weights from gradient norms or from the neural tangent kernel in the Expert's Guide; Gaussian-likelihood weights, among others reviewed by Theodosiou and Rekatsinas 2025), are not the main choice: they add hyperparameters and are harder to reproduce, as Theodosiou and Rekatsinas argue. They are kept as a declared alternative, to be used if a term is seen to dominate the loss during the pilot run. The gradient-norm scheme of the Expert's Guide is the first candidate.

## 6. Success criteria and interpretability

Version 1 asks that the simulated FC match the measured FC. The pointwise error between the simulated and measured BOLD is reported as a complementary metric of the network fit and is not a criterion of success: with a stochastic model, two simulations with identical parameters and different noise do not coincide pointwise, and spectral DCM (Friston et al. 2014; Novelli et al. 2024) fits second-order statistics for that reason.

The FC also varies between simulations, so $\rho_{\mathrm{FC}}$ never reaches 1. It is read against two ceilings: the correlation between the FC of simulations with the same parameters and different seeds, and, if the subject has a second session, the correlation between the FC of both sessions. The dataset has a second session for 88 of its 261 subjects; whether this subject has one has not been confirmed.

The parameters are called interpretable when two conditions hold. First, they are stable across seeds and across the grid of $\lambda$ values. Second, the estimated $\eta_i$ lie inside the bistable range $(-5.35,-2.45)$. Relating the estimated $\eta_i$ to an independent property of the brain, such as the total connection strength of each region, is a next step.

## 7. Limitations and open problems

- The static FC constrains the parameters only weakly, and models with regional parameters on a connectome are known to produce non-unique solutions (Xie et al. 2018). The power spectrum and the dynamic FC are future work.
- First run: $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}$ fell from 0.995 to 0.28 ($\rho_{\mathrm{FC}}\approx0.72$) while $\eta$ moved about $5\cdot10^{-5}$ and $G$ about $5\cdot10^{-6}$, so the fit was made by the network and not by the parameters.
- Direct stochastic simulation (Heun, step 0.1 ms, additive noise on $V$) with homogeneous $\eta=-4.6$ gave $\rho\approx0.03$–$0.045$ over a sweep of $G$. That run used $\tau_m=10$ ms and a noise level not tied to the value of this proposal, so it does not test the present configuration. Between 6.7 and 6.8 times $G_0$ all regions jump together, which global signal regression may remove; this is unconfirmed and requires plotting $r_i(t)$.
- Deferred to the simulation stages: the value of $G_0$ and the useful range of $G$; the check, at $\sigma^2=0.03$ and $\tau_m=1$ ms, that regions switch between states; the convention of the noise level (variance or standard deviation) and the integrator; the scale $\chi$; heterogeneous $\eta_i$ in the generator; and the numerical value of the scale of each physics residual.
- The architecture is the reference of the cited PINNs, not tuned to this problem; the bottleneck of 100 hidden units against 140 outputs is a declared limit.
- Implementation details: centre the simulated BOLD before global signal regression; use an unbiased standard deviation or `torch.corrcoef` in the FC; create $\eta$ and $G$ in the same cell as the optimiser; shift the TR indices if the grid starts at $t=-20$ s. The dataset descriptor states that its FC is a Pearson correlation and mentions no Fisher $z$ transformation.
- Future versions add delays $D_{ij}/c$ and diagnostics with a shuffled connectome and several seeds. About 85% of the time per epoch (about 20 s) is the sequential Balloon loop, which a GPU does not accelerate.

# Idea central (pre-proposal)

Neural mass models of the MPR type (Montbrio-Pazo-Roxin), coupled through the structural
connectome, can reproduce whole-brain fMRI signals. Hypothesis: parameters can be found
that generate the exact population dynamics on the connectome and fit fMRI data.

Sketches: DiagramaPT1.png (architecture), DiagramaPT2.png (loss), DiagramaPT3.png (notation).

## Pipeline (from the sketches)
- PINN: input t -> outputs r_i(t) (rate) and v_i(t) (voltage) for N regions; weights w.
- Autodiff gives dr/dt, dv/dt, substituted into the MPR equations:
  - tau_m r_i' = Delta/(pi tau_m) + 2 r_i v_i
  - tau_m v_i' = v_i^2 + eta_i - (pi tau_m r_i)^2 + tau_m J r_i + tau_m G sum_j C_ij r_j(t - D_ij/v)
  - C_ij = A_ij (.) W_ij, or C_ij = A_ij
- Hemodynamic integrator (balloon): s' = r - kappa s - gamma(f-1); f' = s; tau_h v' = f - v^(1/alpha);
  tau_h q' = f E(f)/E0 - v^(1/alpha) q/v; E(f) = 1-(1-E0)^(1/f). Rest: s=0, f=v=q=1.
- BOLD: y_i = V0[k1(1-q) + k2(1-q/v) + k3(1-v)], k1=4.3 nu0 E0 TE, k2=eps r0 E0 TE, k3=1-eps.
- Y_hat (N x K) -> FC_sim = Corr(rows i, j), N x N.

## Parameters
- theta_F = [eta_i, Delta, J, tau_m]; theta_G = [kappa, gamma, alpha, E0, V0, eps]; theta = [theta_F, theta_G].

## Loss
- Residuals R_r, R_v = LHS - RHS of the MPR equations, at collocation times t_1..t_K.
- L_fisica = 1/(NK) sum_k sum_i [lambda_r R_r^2 + lambda_v R_v^2]
- L_theta = sum_p (theta_p - mu_p)^2 / (2 sigma_p^2) (Gaussian prior; log-normal if positive)
- L_datos = 1 - rho, rho = Corr(a, b), a/b = upper triangle (i<j) of FC_sim / FC_real
  (diagonal is trivial; lower triangle repeats the upper). Compares pattern, not level.
- L = lambda_fisica L_fisica + lambda_datos L_datos + lambda_theta L_theta
- Joint gradient descent on w and theta; repeat.

## Notation (DiagramaPT3)
N regions i=1..N; collocation times K={t_1..t_K}; A connectome; D fibre lengths; tau_ij = D_ij / v.

# Hybrid PINN for neural-mass parameter estimation (v1) — summary from the implementation session

Pasted from the other session; kept in Spanish as received.

## 1. Objetivo
Estimar parámetros de un modelo de cerebro completo para que el BOLD simulado reproduzca la FC en reposo de un sujeto real.
Piezas: conectoma estructural (dMRI), masa neuronal MPR por región, modelo hemodinámico. Problema inverso resuelto con una PINN (PyTorch).

Hipótesis:
- H1: la FC simulada se parece a la FC real más de lo que se parece el conectoma estructural solo.
- H2: con datos sintéticos de parámetros conocidos, el método los recupera.
- H3: simular la MPR directamente con los parámetros estimados, sin la red, reproduce la FC real.

## 2. Datos
- 1000BRAINS v1.1, atlas Desikan-Killiany, N = 70, un sujeto.
- S: conectividad estructural N×N; D: longitudes de fibra en mm (no se usa en v1); FC: Pearson empírica; bold: 300 volúmenes.
- TR = 2.2 s, TE = 30 ms, 3 T.
- Preproceso del dataset: regresión de molestias con GSR, pasa-altas, detrend lineal, sin pasa-banda. Al BOLD simulado también se le aplica GSR.
- Conectoma usado: A = S con diagonal 0 (la autoexcitación ya está en J), normalizado a máximo 1.

## 3. MPR sin retardos (t en s, tau_m = 10 ms)
- tau_m dr_i/dt = Delta/(pi tau_m) + 2 r_i V_i
- tau_m dV_i/dt = V_i^2 + eta_i - (pi tau_m r_i)^2 + tau_m J r_i + tau_m G sum_j A_ij r_j

| Parámetro | v1 |
| --- | --- |
| Delta | 0.7 fijo (Rabuffo) |
| J | 14.5 fijo (Rabuffo) |
| tau_m | 0.01 s fijo |
| eta_i | uno por región, se estima; inicio -4.6 (rango biestable -5.35..-2.45) |
| G | acople global, se estima |
| Retardos | ninguno en v1; luego D_ij/c, c = 4 m/s |

- G0 = 1/(suma media por fila de A).
- Equilibrios de región aislada con eta = -4.6: bajo ≈ 5.7 Hz, frontera inestable ≈ 45 Hz, alto ≈ 101 Hz.

## 4. Hemodinámica
- Balloon-Windkessel (Friston 2000): s, f, nu, q; kappa 0.65, gamma 0.41, tau_h 0.98, alpha 0.32, E0 0.34.
- Entrada: z_i = chi (r_i - rbar_i), con rbar_i = promedio en el tiempo de la región i. Con r crudo el reposo se corre: f* = 1 + rbar/gamma.
- chi fija, 0.1 en entrenamiento (std de z ~ 0.1; si z es grande, f < 0 y explota).
- BOLD: Stephan 2007 ec. 12, V0 = 4, eps = 0.47, vartheta0 = 84.8, varrho0 = 110, TE = 0.03.
- RK4 a mano en PyTorch, diferenciable, sin in-place; dt_h = 0.2 s; reposo inicial s=0, f=nu=q=1; BOLD cada TR. Validado contra scipy.

## 5. PINN
- Una red para todas las regiones: t -> 2N = 140 salidas (r_1..r_N, V_1..V_N).
- 2 capas ocultas de 512, tanh; t escalado a [-1, 1].
- r pasa por softplus; V libre. Derivadas por autodiff respecto a t.
- Entrenables: pesos (varphi), eta (N), G; Adam lr 1e-3; G recortado a >= 0.

## 6. Pérdida
L = lambda_fis L_fis + lambda_datos L_datos + lambda_theta L_theta

- L_fis: K = 1000 instantes aleatorios por época; R_r = tau_m dr/dt - (Delta/(pi tau_m) + 2 r V); R_V = tau_m dV/dt - (V^2 + eta - (pi tau_m r)^2 + tau_m J r + tau_m G sum_j A_ij r_j);
  L_fis = 1/(NK) sum_k sum_i [lambda_rv R_r^2 + (1 - lambda_rv) R_V^2], lambda_rv = 0.5.
  R_r está en Hz y pesa más que R_V; balance pendiente. R_V no debería llegar a 0 (absorbe el ruido).
- L_datos: red en malla regular -> z -> Balloon (RK4) -> BOLD en TR -> GSR -> FC sim (Pearson); comparada con FC real en 2415 pares del triángulo superior:
  L_datos = MSE(FC_sim, FC_real) + (1 - rho_FC). No se compara el BOLD punto a punto (actividad espontánea, unidades distintas).
- L_theta: eta_i ~ N(-4.6, 1^2); G ~ N(G0, (0.5 G0)^2); lambda_theta = 1.
- lambda_datos = 1; lambda_fis = L_datos/L_fis recalculado cada 10 épocas (medido solo en época 0, la física dejó de pesar). Alternativa con fundamento pendiente: lambda_fis = N T / (2 sigma^2).
- Parada: hasta 1000 épocas; cada 50 se revisa eta (< 0.01), G (< 1 %), mejora de L_datos (< 0.005); se detiene ante nan.

## 7. Resultados
- Primera corrida (100 épocas, lambda_fis fijado en época 0): L_datos 0.995 -> 0.28 (rho_FC ≈ 0.72); L_fis 258 -> 0.41; peso de la física ~1 -> ~0.0016.
- eta cambió ~5e-5 y G ~5e-6: el ajuste lo hizo la red, no los parámetros. La red compensa cualquier eta ajustando V, así que el gradiente de eta no tiene dirección consistente. Para que los parámetros se muevan, la física debe pesar en serio.
- Cambios posteriores: lambda_fis recalculado cada 10 épocas; término MSE en L_datos.

## 8. Validación directa (H3)
- MPR sin la red, con eta y G aprendidos, ruido aditivo en V (sigma = 0.5, provisional), paso 0.5 ms; mismo Balloon, BOLD, GSR, FC; chi recalculado para la r simulada.
- Resultado: FC plana, rho ≈ 0.
- Barrido de G (eta = -4.6, sigma = 0.5): rho ≈ 0 de 0 a 6 G0 (0.033 en 6 G0, 0.035 en 6.5 G0); FC media = -0.014 ≈ -1/(N-1) (lo que da la GSR con señales independientes); fracción en estado alto = 0 (todo en ~5.7 Hz temblando por ruido); desde 7 G0 explota.
- Diagnóstico: la FC en reposo de la MPR aparece cuando las regiones saltan entre estado bajo y alto y los saltos se propagan por el conectoma (cascadas, Rabuffo). Con estos sigma y G no hay saltos. La explosión en 7 G0 es numérica: hubs en estado alto con oscilaciones rápidas que Euler no resuelve (ni con 0.05 ms). Euler a 0.5 ms sacaba artificialmente regiones del estado alto.
- Solo en la validación se cambió a Heun estocástico, paso 0.1 ms (estable). El entrenamiento no se tocó: la MPR solo aparece en los residuos.
- Pendiente: barrido con Heun en 6.5-10 G0 buscando saltos y rho > 0.
